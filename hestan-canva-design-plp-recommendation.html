What’s the Difference Between a Stockpot and a Rondeau?

A stockpot is tall and narrow. Its height keeps evaporation slow, which is what you want for stock, soup, chili and boiling pasta. A rondeau is wide and shallow with two loop handles. All that surface area lets you sear meat without crowding, reduce sauces quickly and braise in the oven. Many cooks own one of each: the stockpot for liquids, the rondeau for browning and braising.

Which Size Stockpot Do I Need?

An 8-quart stockpot covers most households: a pound of pasta, a pot of chili or soup for six. Go up to 12 quarts for stock from whole bones, canning or cooking for a crowd. For everyday soups, grains and smaller batches, a 5- to 6-quart soup pot or rondeau is easier to lift and store.

Copper, Titanium or Stainless: Which Is Best?

NanoBond® adds thousands of titanium nano-layers to stainless steel, so it resists scratches, stains and salt pitting and can go in the dishwasher. CopperBond® Aura uses 1.5mm copper for the fastest, most precise heat response, ideal for delicate sauces and braises. ProBond Luxe® is classic polished tri-ply stainless, the best value for everyday cooking. All three share Hestan’s ProCore™ aluminum core for even heat.
